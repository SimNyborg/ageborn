## 2. Per-game findings

### Age of War 1 and 2, Army of Ages (the baseline)
- **Why it was fun:** a visible arc from clubs to lasers within one match. Evolution changes both the units and the look of the base. You face a constant choice between spending gold on units now, on turrets, or holding XP to evolve. The special attack gives a "save the day" moment. The only control is clicking buttons at the top.
- **Degenerate strategies:** stacking cheap units at your own base ("200+ slingshotters"), spamming the ion cannon, or sitting behind max turrets until you can afford a wave of super soldiers. As one iOS reviewer put it, "once you have the max level turrets you just sit there until you have enough money to spam."
- **Stalemates:** on Hard, players describe "an infinite battle" at the enemy base because the AI keeps regenerating and bunching up. One player quit at 9861/10000 base damage after 40 minutes.
- **Difficulty tuning:** the most common AoW2 complaint is the large gap between Easy and Normal. Insane mode felt like trial and error.
- **Evolution penalty:** in Army of Ages, evolving deletes your units and turrets. That was the review's main criticism.
- **Wishes:** multiplayer (in AoW2 iOS reviews), a mute button, more ages and more content. Players also found strategic variety thin: "just a race to higher evolution."

### Stick War: Legacy
- **Controls:** tap unit buttons. A global stance (Attack / Defend / Garrison) moves the whole army. You can **take control of one unit**, and it then fights better and faster than the AI. This one extra verb is the franchise's signature and the part players single out as fun.
- **Economy:** you train miners, so every early decision trades economy against army.
- **Modes:** campaign, tournaments, Endless Deads (zombie survival), missions. Skins act as both cosmetics and stat boosters.
- **Complaints:** late content effectively requires a maxed armory and skins; gems come slowly (5-15 per mission); weekly missions sit behind a $12 paywall; saves have corrupted; there is no online PvP. Despite this it keeps a 4.7-4.8 rating and 100M+ downloads, which suggests the core loop can carry weak monetization.

### Stick War: Saga (the franchise's attempt at a Clash-style PvP meta)
- **Features:** 1v1 and 2v2 PvP, battle decks, generals, skins, emotes, voice lines, statues, and a replay system with pause, rewind and multiple viewpoints. Replays are good for learning and for making clips.
- **Backlash:** generals sold for real money gave power while marketing said "no pay for power". The deck unlock tree matched players against opponents with more cards. Free daily PvE battles were removed and gem rewards reduced. Both moves were read as taking something away from players.
- **Lesson:** PvP players forgive a lot, but not paid power or removed freebies.

### The Battle Cats
- **Battle loop:** a lineup of up to 10 units in two rows of five, each with its own cooldown. The **Worker Cat** is an in-battle upgrade that raises money income (about $170/s at level 1, each level about 5% faster). The **Cat Cannon** is a base special charged over 10 bars. Upgrades between battles raise starting money, wallet size and bounty.
- **Deckbuilding depth:** 200+ "Cat Combos". Specific cats in the top row give small, medium or large bonuses such as attack up, starting money, cannon recharge or freeze duration. Collection breadth pays off without raw stat inflation.
- **Collection:** six rarity tiers from Normal to Legend Rare. A free daily ticket goes to the Normal capsule. Rare capsule: 150 Cat Food single, 1,500 for 11 draws, 5% Uber Rare, with events that guarantee an Uber on the 11th draw. Duplicates become "+levels" past the level cap, so they are never worthless. The gacha is seeded per account, and the community plans its pulls around that.
- **Complaints:** energy spent on failed attempts ("I practically wasted all of my energy"), walls that seem to require a specific Uber, treasure grind, and progress lost when moving device because saves are manual.
- **Growth:** humour plus weird cute designs is highly clippable. One YouTube influencer campaign produced 12.3M views and +183% installs over three days.

### Cartoon Wars and Kingdom Wars (mobile clones)
- **Cartoon Wars:** mana spawns units, gold upgrades them, gems unlock premium units, and the tower has an auto-crossbow. Critics called it addictive but shallow, with progress gated by grind or payment.
- **Kingdom Wars:** 27M downloads on content volume (400+ stages, 200+ units). The loudest complaints are about **quality-of-life features being taken away**. When the speed button was removed, PvP went from 1-3 minutes to about 15. Speed-up and auto mode were later moved behind a paywall. Players also complained about ads after every other level. Being an obvious Battle Cats copy was itself a complaint.

### Castle Crush
- A high store rating (4.8, 80K) hides deep distrust (Metacritic users 3.3/10). The themes are undisclosed bots, card draw felt as rigged, matchmaking that seems to push losing streaks, and pay-to-win.
- **Lesson:** once players believe the game is rigged, every loss gets blamed on the game. Transparency is a design feature.

### Rise of Legions / Crystal Clash
- A good design (82% positive, a 9/10 review) died from **player population**, not quality. Crystal Clash, focused on 2v2, fell from a 1,145 concurrent-player peak to a 186 monthly average within one month and has about 3 players today.
- **Lesson:** a small studio should never make PvP the only fun thing to do. PvE, bots and asynchronous ghosts must carry the game until real players arrive.

### Clash Royale (reference for the meta layer)
- 3-minute matches with overtime; 8-card deck with 4 in hand; elixir capped at 10 with double elixir in the final minute. About $1B in year one.
- Deconstructor of Fun credits its retention to sessions of any length (from 5 seconds to hours), new cards dripped every 2-3 months, balance where "a new card never replaces an old one", and clans.
- Chest timers, queues and keys were **removed in April 2025** so players could battle and get rewards immediately. Lucky Drops came in 2024 and were replaced by Lucky Chests. Players say the problem with boxes is "the low chances of getting good rewards", not the boxes themselves.

### Browser portal constraints (CrazyGames requirements)
- Initial download of 50MB or less, and 20MB or less to qualify for the mobile homepage.
- Gameplay reachable within about 20 seconds.
- Must run on a 4GB-RAM Chromebook. Safari support is mandatory. Touch, mouse and keyboard all required.

## 3. Cross-genre complaint list (ranked by how many titles show it)

1. **Paid or locked power** (Saga generals, Cartoon Wars, Castle Crush, Grow Empire, AoW mobile generals at $6).
2. **Currency starvation and grind** ("gem poor", 5-15 gems per mission, treasure grinding).
3. **Difficulty cliffs** (AoW2 Easy vs Normal, Battle Cats walls, Stick War late game).
4. **Stalemates and repetitive endgame** (infinite battles at the base, turtling with turrets, "spam skirmishers or cavalry").
5. **Suspected rigging, undisclosed bots, unfair matchmaking** (Castle Crush, Saga deck tree, Rise of Legions deck tiers).
6. **Free features taken away** (speed button, auto mode, daily PvE, reduced rewards).
7. **Intrusive ads** (Kingdom Wars2, AoW mobile).
8. **Lost progress** (Battle Cats device transfer, Stick War save corruption, Grow Empire Game Center).
9. **Dominant single strategies** (ion cannon, slingshot stacking, anti-armor units too strong in AoW2).
10. **Missing multiplayer** in single-player titles, and **lag** in multiplayer ones.
11. **Punishing evolution** (Army of Ages wipes your army).
12. **UI covering the battlefield on touch screens** (Warlords).

## 4. Top 10 design lessons for our game

**1. Readable silhouettes and humour beat art fidelity.**
Stick figures reached 100M+ downloads and weird cats 110M. Art made by code works if every unit has a distinct outline, a clear age palette and exaggerated attack animations (squash and stretch, hit-flash, knockback, screen shake).
*Do:* define each unit's look as data (a body template plus parts, colours and animation curves) so it can be swapped for sprites or Spine files later. Give units comic personality through death poses, idle wiggles and sound.

**2. Give the player one more verb than "spawn".**
Stick War's single-unit control and army stances are what players single out as fun, and Age of War's special attack is its most dramatic moment.
*Do:* v1 gets a global stance (Advance / Hold / Fall back to turrets), a per-age special ability with a visible charge meter (like the Cat Cannon), and a tap-to-rally line. Possessing a hero unit can be a v2 feature.

**3. Make evolving a high-stakes choice, never a penalty.**
Army of Ages' wipe on evolve was its most hated mechanic. Age of War 2 was called "a race to higher evolution".
*Do:* units already on the field survive evolution. Evolving costs XP plus a short vulnerability window, such as a pause in spawning or a turret refit time, and grants a dramatic transformation moment on the base. Each age should change the tactics, for example by adding flying units or a new counter type, not only the numbers.

**4. Put an economy decision inside the battle.**
Stick War's miners and Battle Cats' Worker Cat create the greed-versus-safety tension that keeps each match interesting.
*Do:* add an upgradable income building or "worker" track each age, so players choose between raising income and spending now. This also makes rushes and economic play viable styles.

**5. Design out stalemates with a match clock.**
The most common frustration in Age of War 2 was endless battles at the enemy base. Clash Royale solves this with 3 minutes plus double elixir. Kingdom Wars PvP at 15 minutes drew complaints.
*Do:* target 4-7 minutes for PvP-style matches. After a set time, turn on sudden death: double income, bases taking passive damage, and turrets weakening. Add diminishing returns or a soft population cap to stop mass-stacking one unit. Campaign levels can run longer but need the same escalation.

**6. Decks per age, built on counters and small synergies, not power creep.**
Battle Cats' 200+ combos and Clash Royale's "a new card never replaces an old one" keep collections meaningful.
*Do:* each age deck holds about 4-5 units plus 2 turrets. Every unit carries tags (melee, ranged, armored, air, siege, support) and a clear counter table. Add light synergy bonuses (for example "3 ranged: +10% range"). Keep all stats in JSON and log bot-versus-bot win rates for balancing.

**7. Fair, generous progression with no energy system.**
Energy systems, gem starvation and difficulty walls are the loudest complaints across the genre. Since cases never cost money, there is no reason to starve players.
*Do:* no stamina system. Upgrades should make the game easier without being required. Ranked or "fair" modes normalise unit levels (Clash-style tournament levels), so skill decides. Smooth the difficulty curve (no Easy-to-Normal cliff) and offer optional hard modifiers for veterans.

**8. Make case opening exciting, transparent and generous.**
Clash Royale players blame poor odds, not the boxes, and Supercell removed timers entirely. Battle Cats' guaranteed Uber draws and duplicates turning into +levels keep pulls meaningful.
*Do:* earn cases through play without timers or keys. Show the odds and a pity counter (for example an Epic within 10 cases and a Legendary within 40). Duplicates convert to upgrade shards or +levels. Build tension in the reveal: rarity colour telegraphed by shake and glow, a slow-down, a pause before legendaries, and a sound cue for each tier. Cosmetic skins carry most of the prestige. Keep all of this away from real-money paths so it never becomes gambling.

**9. PvE and honest bots must carry the game.**
Crystal Clash's collapse shows that PvP-only indie games die. Castle Crush's hidden bots destroyed player trust.
*Do:* ship a campaign across all ages, daily and weekly challenge modifiers, an endless survival mode (like Stick War's Endless Deads) and a bot ladder. Bots should be labelled clearly and have personalities (Rusher, Turtler, Eco-greed, Spammer) and fair rules, meaning no hidden resource cheats. Later, add ghost opponents built from recorded player decks, also labelled. Add replays; they build trust and double as marketing clips.

**10. Respect the player's time and trust.**
Removing the speed button, moving auto mode behind a paywall, cutting free rewards and losing saves all caused lasting damage.
*Do:*
- Add a 1x/2x/3x speed toggle, pause, separate music and SFX volume, and auto-save to localStorage with a save export/import code.
- Never take a free feature away.
- Build for the web from day one: 20MB or less initial load, gameplay within 20 seconds, runs on a 4GB Chromebook, Safari and touch support.
- Keep controls away from the lane on phones; Warlords showed what happens when icons cover the battlefield.

## 5. Concrete v1 targets

- **Match:** one lane, 5-6 ages, a deck per age (about 4-5 units and 2 turrets), a per-age special, stance commands, an in-battle income track, sudden death after about 5 minutes, and a soft population cap.
- **Modes:** a campaign of about 30+ levels spread over the ages, a labelled bot ladder with 4 personalities, endless survival, and daily challenges.
- **Meta:** a profile, collection screen, rarity tiers from Common to Legendary, case opening without timers but with pity, duplicates converted to upgrade shards, cosmetic skins, and normalised levels in "fair" matches.
- **Quality of life:** speed toggle, pause, volume sliders, auto-save plus export, and odds shown on every case.
- **Architecture:** all unit, turret and age data in JSON; a renderer interface so procedural drawing can be swapped for sprites later; a deterministic simulation (needed for replays and later server-side PvP).
